## Scientific claims the driver qualifies or does not adopt

The audit calls the true-parent/rollout gap a signature of exposure bias and
suggests this trial separates exposure bias from capacity/objective failure.
The evidence establishes one-step deficits AND extra accumulated deficits;
it does not uniquely identify either cause. Teacher-forced maximum-likelihood
training can in principle learn correct conditionals with adequate data/model/
optimization. Thus 'teacher-forced NLL can never see it' is too absolute as a
general impossibility claim. This experiment compares two finite training
procedures; a null cannot prove that an entire model family is unlearnable.
The plan's terminal closure is a resource/prioritization decision, not that proof.

Likewise, '1000 updates will not plausibly fix it' and 'both-fail is the modal
outcome' are the auditor's qualitative forecasts, not measured probabilities.
Do not promise success; retain the fixed evaluation and all results. The
gradient-ratio rule balances estimated norms, not true expected signal. A
finite operational screen cannot remove high-dimensional estimator risk.

The auditor proposes coarse present posterior -> prior fine IC phases ->
forward zoom if the repair fails. This is NOT adopted as an automatic fallback.
It might supply dynamically evolved fine structure, but by itself does not
condition LG structure on MW/M31/M33 observations, solve the fine present-field
posterior, or demonstrate the user's requested information gain. Prior phases
are allowed for unconstrained modes, not an excuse to omit available LG data.
Changing the route/scope requires a concrete observation-conditioned design
and explicit user decision. The current present-field-first goal is preserved.

The audit correctly warns that global environment and selection remain major
unknowns even if this local prior improves. No wording in the corrected plan
promotes the composite proxy, necessary budgets or selected native positives
to those missing laws. No halo labels are assigned to generated fields by
